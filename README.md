# Understanding Attrition Drivers for Employee Retention

## Overview
Employee attrition can negatively impact productivity, team stability, and overall company performance.  
This project explores employee data to understand patterns associated with attrition and to identify factors that may help explain why employees leave the company.

The analysis is descriptive and focuses on understanding trends and differences between employees who leave and those who stay.

---

## Business Questions
The analysis addresses the following questions:

- Which departments have the highest attrition rates?
- Is there a difference in income between employees who leave and those who stay?
- How is overtime related to employee attrition?
- Do employees who leave tend to have fewer years at the company?
- Do combinations of factors, such as overtime and low income, appear frequently among employees who leave?

---

## Key Findings
- Sales has the highest attrition rate among departments.
- Employees who leave the company tend to earn less than those who stay.
- Overtime is more common among employees who leave.
- A large share of employees who leave experience both overtime and below-average income.
- Employees who leave the company generally have fewer years of tenure.

---

## Business Interpretation
The results suggest that employee attrition is associated with multiple factors rather than a single cause.  
Lower income, higher overtime, and shorter tenure appear more frequently among employees who leave.

In departments such as Sales, higher attrition does not seem to be explained by income alone, indicating that workload or role specific pressures may also contribute to attrition.

---

## Recommendations
Based on the findings, the following actions could help improve employee retention:

- Review overtime practices, especially in departments with higher attrition.
- Reassess compensation levels for lower-income employees, particularly those working overtime.
- Strengthen onboarding and early support programs, as attrition is more common during the first years at the company.
